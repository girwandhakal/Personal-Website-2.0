WorthIt: a pause before checkout

A cart advisor that offers a second look at a purchase, with asynchronous product loading and local purchase history.

The problem

By checkout, most shopping interfaces are optimized for finishing the purchase. WorthIt creates a brief moment to reconsider items that may duplicate a recent purchase or have a supported cheaper alternative. The extension works with Amazon and Walmart carts and was built for the fintech track at Hacklanta 2026.

The interaction is deliberately bounded: it can hold the first checkout click once, highlight at most three items, and let the shopper continue. It never changes the cart or completes checkout for the user.

How the extension works

Normalize retailer data

Retailer adapters turn cart rows into a shared data shape. The cart review activates on the cart route even though the content script must account for single-page navigation. Assessment logic and the presentation then work from normalized item data instead of retailer-specific markup.

Ask before assuming

The review considers supported store-brand swaps, similar recent purchases, novelty items, and fixed food-label concerns. A duplicate-looking purchase prompts a clarification, such as whether it is a replacement or a gift, rather than treating similarity as proof the purchase is unnecessary.

Respond before enrichment finishes

Asynchronous product-data loading lets the initial review appear before slower enrichment finishes. The résumé benchmark records initial review latency falling from 4 seconds to 0.27 seconds, a 93% reduction, in testing. That is an initial-response measurement, not a guarantee that every product fact has loaded in 0.27 seconds. The Walmart entrypoint also caches product-label facts locally for repeat visits.

Keep history local

Purchase history stays in IndexedDB. Optional Gmail receipt import uses read-only access, parses receipts locally, and asks the user to review proposed records before saving them. Email bodies are not retained. Retailer content scripts do not need access to bank accounts, payment fields, or a broad browsing history.

Important product decisions

The extension offers a short recap and a separate full analysis with item-level findings and sources. This keeps the checkout interruption small while preserving a path to the supporting detail.

An optional investment chart illustrates what the cart's potential savings could become. It is hypothetical, can be turned off, and does not use personal financial accounts. Optional AI wording polish receives placeholders rather than actual names, prices, or purchase history; assessment facts remain structured.

Local processing removes the need for a backend in the central assessment flow, but creates a clear tradeoff: history is tied to the device. Public extension distribution and Gmail OAuth verification are separate release concerns from a working unpacked hackathon build.

What I learned

The project demonstrates a complete checkout-review loop and a measured improvement in initial responsiveness. Browser fixtures and automated tests cover repeatable retailer interactions; live retailer pages and live Gmail access still require their own verification.

The useful engineering lesson is to separate the first response from enrichment. A shopper can start understanding the review while slower product details arrive, as long as the interface does not present unknown information as established fact.
