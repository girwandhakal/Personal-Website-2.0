What should a planner replace first?

A device nearing the end of its life raises a few questions. Is it still supported? Where is it? What would it cost to replace? We wanted planners to be able to look at those together when deciding which equipment to replace first.

We built the project as a team at UA Innovate. I worked on the assistant, brought predictive risk scores into the dashboard, and mapped more than 1,000 devices. We took second place.

What we built into the dashboard

Organize the planning questions

We organized the Streamlit dashboard around the decisions someone might be making, from checking geographic risk and asset health to forecasting and prioritizing investment. Pandas gives the pages a shared dataset, and Plotly makes the views interactive.

Connect risk with cost

A high risk score is one part of a replacement decision. Location, support status, and cost can change the priority. We built a budget simulator to explore that and added data upload, validation, and export.

Give the assistant page context

I gave our assistant, Southern Spark, the current page and a summary of its data. Depending on which columns exist, that can include row counts, risk distribution, devices by state, replacement costs, and support status. GPT-4o gets that context and the conversation history, then streams its reply.

How much the assistant could see

The summary keeps the amount of data sent to the assistant manageable. It can explain patterns on the current page. Questions about a particular device would need a way to look up individual records.

We used forecasts and what-if views to explore how risk might change. That was part of the hackathon demo. Using those forecasts for maintenance decisions would take separate validation.

What I'd add for device-level questions

The demo let someone move from the risk map to a forecast or a budget question without switching tools.

Building Southern Spark made me pay attention to exactly what the model sees. If I wanted it to explain why a single device was flagged, I'd first give it a reliable way to fetch that device's record.
