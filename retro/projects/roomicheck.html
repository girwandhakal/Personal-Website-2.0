The problem

Roommate preferences are difficult to capture in a fixed checklist. A single response about guests or cleanliness can imply several preferences, but it can also leave important details unresolved. RoomiCheck explores an adaptive interview that turns those answers into a structured co-living profile.

As Software Engineering Lead from December 2025 to May 2026, I led engineering, built and deployed a FastAPI questionnaire, and piloted it with more than 50 users. The team secured $2,500 in non-dilutive funding through RiverPitch 2025 and the 2026 Aldag Entrepreneurship Competition. We also ran A/B tests to compare questionnaire strategies; no numerical winning effect is claimed here.

Separating interpretation from control

Separate interpretation from scoring

The core design separates two responsibilities. A language model interprets a sanitized written answer and returns bounded labels, confidence, and supporting evidence. Backend code owns the profile arithmetic and questionnaire progression. This keeps the conversation flexible without making the model the final scoring authority.

Follow the information gaps

The public repository develops this into an eight-dimension co-living profile. Scenario prompts cover topics such as cleanliness, sleeping and studying, guests, boundaries, and conflict communication. Missing or low-confidence information guides follow-up selection, rather than asking every participant the same unnecessary questions.

Validate every answer

API contracts reject unexpected fields, bound answer lengths and scale values, and require exactly one answer form. Answer submissions carry an idempotency key. Model extraction contracts retain supporting quotes and distinguish direct from inferred evidence, making the profile more inspectable than a single opaque summary.

Handle privacy and availability

Privacy handling redacts common identifiers and withholds sensitive-topic answers before model calls. The prototype also includes deterministic scoring and curated follow-ups for the non-AI path. These controls make provider availability a workflow concern rather than a reason to trust an unvalidated answer.

Making the product boundary clear

The current public milestone creates a profile; it does not yet rank or match roommates. That distinction matters. A profile can document evidence and uncertainty, while a matching system requires additional decisions about how to compare people and evaluate compatibility.

The codebase contains an internal session audit view with questions, responses, profile snapshots, AI runs, and timeline events. This supports examining how an assessment arrived at a result, including unresolved gaps, rather than relying only on the final paragraph.

The repository reflects continued prototype development. Its present architecture is described here separately from the dated leadership and pilot achievements above.

What I took away

The pilot and funded prototype established a practical way to collect richer preferences than a static form. The engineering work centers on structured evidence, bounded inference, and a questionnaire that can recover from uncertainty.

A conversational interface still needs explicit rules. Letting the model phrase and interpret answers is useful; giving it unchecked authority over scoring, completion, or compatibility would make the system much harder to explain and evaluate.
