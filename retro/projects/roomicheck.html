Where a checklist falls short

A checklist can ask how often you have guests. A written answer might tell you that weekends are fine but leave overnight visits unclear. With RoomiCheck, I wanted the next question to depend on what the student had already told us.

I led engineering from December 2025 to May 2026, built and deployed the FastAPI questionnaire, and helped pilot it with more than 50 users. We ran A/B tests on interview strategies and secured $2,500 in non-dilutive funding through RiverPitch 2025 and the 2026 Aldag Entrepreneurship Competition.

How I used the model without handing it the scores

Separate interpretation from scoring

I gave the model one job. It reads a sanitized answer and returns labels from a fixed set, with evidence and a confidence estimate. Backend rules calculate the scores and decide what the questionnaire asks next.

Follow the information gaps

The public prototype builds a profile across eight dimensions, including cleanliness, sleep and study habits, guests, boundaries, and conflict. Missing or uncertain details steer the follow-ups, so people can skip questions they've already answered in substance.

Validate every answer

The API checks lengths, scale ranges, and unexpected fields, and accepts one answer form at a time. Submissions carry a key so retries can be recognized. I keep supporting quotes and distinguish what the student said directly from what the model inferred.

Handle privacy and availability

We redact common identifiers and keep sensitive-topic answers out of model calls. If the provider is unavailable, the prototype can still score answers with rules and use curated follow-ups.

What the profile can tell us

The public milestone ends with a co-living profile. To turn it into roommate matching, we'd need to decide how to compare two profiles and how to test whether those comparisons predict compatibility.

I can open an internal session view to see questions, answers, profile snapshots, model runs, and timeline events. When a result looks off, I can trace where it came from and see which questions were still open.

The repository covers continued prototype work beyond the leadership period and pilot.

What I learned from incomplete answers

The pilot let us try an interview that could gather more detail than a fixed form and carry that detail into a profile with evidence attached.

I learned to treat uncertainty as a reason to ask a better question. If someone reviewing the profile can see the answer that led to a score and what's still missing, they have something to work with.
