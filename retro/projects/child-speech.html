What I was trying to measure

At the Alabama Life Research Institute, I study how children communicate. I was first author on a paper we submitted for peer review that looked at communication features across more than 60,000 child utterances. In the speech-act project, I prepare transcripts for a pretrained conditional random field model using INCA-A codes, then compare late-talking and typically developing groups.

Children pause, repeat themselves, and start over. A recognizer's top transcript can miss a word that appears in one of its alternatives. That gave me a second question alongside the speech-act work. Could I build a dataset and use those alternatives to recover some of what the recognizer missed?

How I put the dataset together

Resolve the participants

I join a TalkBank search export, Redivis metadata, local CHAT transcripts, and linked media. Before picking any utterances, I resolve who's speaking. I keep IDs separate by corpus, preserve leading zeros, and log why I exclude an ambiguous or conflicting identity.

Define a usable utterance

I use target-child turns with valid timestamps and cleaned reference text. I use those same rows when counting turns, measuring their length, and cutting the audio, so the numbers and clips describe the same data.

Limit unequal contributions

A child recorded over several visits can contribute far more speech than someone recorded once. I set a configurable cap using the cross-sectional distribution and sample whole transcripts with a repeatable seed per child. That keeps me from quietly clipping a long transcript or favoring the earliest visits.

Balance at the child level

I calculate mean utterance length in words and balance at the child level. I keep group mean differences alongside the significance checks so I can see how far apart the groups are even when a test comes back nonsignificant.

Export an auditable handoff

I export the selected turns with canonical child IDs, raw and cleaned text, timestamps, and audio paths. I keep input checksums, exclusion logs, cap settings, and automated audio checks with the export so I can trace a clip back to the selection that produced it.

Giving the model more than one transcript

Keep alternative transcripts

In gensec-asr I generate a deterministic Whisper transcript and sample ten alternatives. After normalization and deduplication, I keep the baseline and up to four alternatives. FLAN-T5 can pull words from several candidates to make a correction.

Resume expensive stages

I run six SLURM stages from reference import and candidate generation through example construction, training and prediction, evaluation, and child-level analysis. Data and config fingerprints tell me when to reuse work or rerun it. I archive the previous evaluation when its replacement is ready so an upstream failure leaves the earlier results available.

Preserve human references

I keep the finalized human reference from the dataset and normalize the prediction separately. I want a scoring change to affect the comparison without changing the words the model is supposed to recover.

Why I changed the split

Split by child, not transcript

When earlier scores looked unusually good, I checked who was in each split. Different recordings of the same child could land on either side. I now keep all recordings for each child together, stratify by analysis group, and test for overlap, missing identities, complete coverage, and independence from row order.

Keep earlier scores in context

An earlier 90,000-utterance experiment brought word error rate from 17.2 to 14.4. Those numbers came from the old setup. I'd rerun the downstream stages before reporting a score under the child-isolated split.

Document the limits

I keep notes on differences in corpus composition and which children appeared across the earlier transcript splits. Those details affect what I can say about how the model would do on a child it has never heard.

Remove a harmful cleaner

I tried removing repeated phrases from generated text. In the audit, that made 61 predictions worse and 15 better, adding 96 word errors overall. I removed the cleaner and used generation controls to address looping while keeping real repetitions in the speech.

What I can trace back to the data

I ended up with a workflow I can rerun from child identity and clip selection through speech correction and evaluation. I can trace which audio and reference text went into a result.

I now decide who needs to be held out before I decide how to split the rows. For this question, that means keeping the child together from dataset construction through evaluation.
