The research problem

My work at the Alabama Life Research Institute studies childhood communication using language data and machine learning. I first-authored a paper, submitted for peer review, evaluating communicative features across more than 60,000 child utterances. The associated speech-act repository prepares transcript data for a pretrained conditional random field model using the INCA-A coding scheme and analyzes late-talking and typically developing groups.

Speech recognition adds another source of uncertainty. Child speech is often disfluent, and a recognizer's top transcript can discard a useful alternative. The research therefore spans three connected codebases: speech-act analysis, dataset preparation, and generative speech error correction.

Building the dataset before training

Resolve the participants

asr-dataset-pipelines connects a TalkBank search export, Redivis transcript metadata, local CHAT archives, and linked media. It resolves participants before selecting utterances. IDs are namespaced by corpus, leading zeros are preserved, and ambiguous or conflicting identities are excluded with a recorded reason.

Define a usable utterance

Only usable target-child turns with a valid timestamp interval and nonempty cleaned reference text enter the pool. Selection uses those same rows for counts, utterance-length calculations, and audio clips, avoiding different definitions of the dataset at different stages.

Limit unequal contributions

Longitudinal participants can contribute much more speech than cross-sectional participants. The pipeline calculates a configurable contribution cap from the cross-sectional distribution and selects whole transcripts with deterministic per-child seeds. It does not silently truncate an oversized transcript or automatically prefer the earliest visits.

Balance at the child level

Mean length of utterance is computed consistently in words. Balancing operates at the child level, and reports retain the group mean difference as well as significance diagnostics. A nonsignificant difference is not treated as proof the groups are equivalent.

Export an auditable handoff

The final handoff includes exact selected utterances, canonical child IDs, cleaned and raw text, timestamps, and linked audio paths. Input checksums, exclusion logs, cap settings, and automated audio validation make the selection auditable.

Generative speech error correction

Keep alternative transcripts

gensec-asr decodes a deterministic Whisper baseline and samples ten alternatives per clip. After normalization and deduplication, it retains the baseline and up to four alternatives. FLAN-T5 then reads these candidates and predicts a corrected transcript. The model can draw useful words from more than one candidate instead of merely accepting the top output.

Resume expensive stages

A six-stage SLURM workflow imports reference pairs, generates candidates, constructs examples, trains and predicts, evaluates, and produces child-level analysis. Expensive stages reuse valid cached work; data fingerprints and configuration signatures invalidate stale training or predictions. Previous evaluation artifacts are archived when replacement results are ready, so a failed upstream job does not erase the earlier run.

Preserve human references

Ground-truth text belongs to the upstream dataset. The downstream pipeline preserves finalized references unchanged and normalizes model predictions separately. That boundary prevents scoring convenience from quietly altering the target.

Evaluation changed the project

Split by child, not transcript

I caught leakage that made earlier results look stronger than they were. Holding out a transcript is insufficient when a different recording of the same child appears in training. The revised splitter keeps all recordings of each child in one partition, stratifies by analysis group, and tests for overlap, complete coverage, missing identities, and row-order independence.

Keep earlier scores in context

An earlier experiment recorded in my résumé reduced word error rate from 17.2 to 14.4 on a 90,000-utterance setup. That historical result is not a score for the revised child-isolated methodology. The repository explicitly requires regenerated downstream results before reporting new benchmark numbers.

Document the limits

The historical child-level analysis also documents corpus-composition differences and children shared across earlier transcript-based splits. I keep those limitations attached to the results rather than promoting an old table as evidence of generalization to unseen children.

Remove a harmful cleaner

Another experiment removed repeated phrases after generation. It damaged legitimate disfluency: in the recorded audit it worsened 61 predictions and improved 15, adding 96 word errors overall. The cleaner was removed; generation controls address pathological looping without rewriting real repetitions in the reference speech.

What this work contributes

The contribution is both modeling and research infrastructure: reproducible selection, explicit speaker identity, traceable audio/reference pairs, resumable training, and evaluation that respects the unit of generalization.

The central lesson: a better score is not enough. I need to know which children, transcripts, preprocessing rules, and model settings produced it—and whether that evidence answers the research question.
